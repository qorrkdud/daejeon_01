Frequently Asked Questions
How do I know if I have sensitive skin?
"
Sensitive skin
is skin that is more reactive than usual,"
Jessie Cheung
, MD, a board-certified dermatologist based in Willowbrook, Chicago, previously
told
Allure
.
This means your skin can be easily irritated by the elements, air pollution, hormones, or, you guessed it, topical skin care products. "If [you find yourself being] cautious with trying new skin care or find that you're frequently battling red, flaky, itchy, or bumpy skin, then you probably have sensitive skin," Dr. Cheung said. Because "sensitive skin" isn't classified as a medical condition and is instead used as a descriptor, it can be tricky to know whether you have it. Ask your board-certified dermatologist for tailored advice if you suspect you have sensitive skin—whether you’ve experienced it for years, or only for the first time.
How should I choose a cleanser for sensitive skin?
"You want to use gentle, hydrating cleansers that remove impurities without stripping the skin barrier of all its natural moisturizing factors," explains
Adam Friedman
, MD, a Washington, DC-based dermatologist. Before you wash your face, seek out products with as short an ingredient list as possible, says
Brandith Irwin
, MD, a board-certified dermatologist based in New York City. "For sensitive skin, specifically, you want products that are
free of artificial fragrances
, parabens, and phthalates."
Ingredients you should look out for: hydrating, soothing, and moisture-sealing ingredients, such as tried-and-true hyaluronic acid, oatmeal, and aloe vera, to calm redness, says
Morgan Rabach, MD
, a board-certified dermatologist based in New York City. She also recommends using a pH-balanced cleanser, which is closer to the natural skin pH of around 5 to 5.5.
What ingredients should I avoid if I have sensitive skin?
People with sensitive skin should avoid
sulfates
,
exfoliants
, retinoids, isopropyl alcohol, glycolic, and salicylic acids—to name a few, says
Amy B. Lewis
, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in New York City. However, your lifestyle habits can play a major role in red, irritated skin. "Some patients think they're sensitive because they're red and dry, but really they're vaping, or smoking cigarettes or weed—so their skin isn't as resilient as it used to be,"
adds
Jeanine Downie
, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Montclair, New Jersey.
It’s important to note that sensitive skin isn't one-size-fits-all—you might even find your triggers inconsistent with conventional wisdom. Therefore, paying close attention to your skin after using any new products, and most importantly, talking to your dermatologist, can make all the difference in achieving healthier, clearer skin.
How often should sensitive skin types cleanse?
Even if you have sensitive skin, cleansing morning and night is essential for keeping your pores clean—just make sure the face wash you use is gentle enough to use twice daily. That being said,
Anna Karp, MD
, a board-certified dermatologist based in New York City, recommends that severely reactive skin types cleanse with a gentle cleanser at night, such as one of the picks above, and simply rinse with lukewarm water in the morning.
Pro tip: “Only cleanse for half a minute, and avoid cloths, textured sponges, and exfoliating brushes,” adds Dr. Rabach. Beyond cleansing, Dr. Karp suggests keeping your
skin-care routine
short and sweet, using only a moisturizer and sunscreen during daylight hours.
